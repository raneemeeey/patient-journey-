Flexor Tendon Injury — Zone 5

A step-by-step patient journey explaining what may happen before, during and after surgical repair of a flexor tendon injury.

Understanding Before Surgery Surgery Day Procedure Complications Wound Care Recovery Discharge Follow-up Life After
01

Understanding the Injury

A flexor tendon is a strong tissue that helps bend the fingers and wrist. A cut or other injury can damage the tendon and make normal finger movement difficult.

What is Zone 5?

Zone 5 generally refers to the region of the flexor tendons around the wrist and forearm on the palm side.

Why might surgery be needed?

When a tendon has been significantly divided, surgical repair may be recommended so that the tendon ends can be brought back together.

02

Before Surgery

Before an operation, your healthcare team assesses the injury and prepares you for the procedure. Exact preparation depends on the injury and your individual circumstances.

Assessment

  • Examination of the hand and fingers
  • Assessment of movement and sensation
  • Evaluation of the wound and surrounding structures

Medical Preparation

  • Review of medical history
  • Medication review
  • Anaesthesia discussion
  • Instructions about eating and drinking
03

The Day of Surgery

On the day of the operation, the healthcare team confirms the planned procedure and makes sure you are ready for surgery.

Arrival

You check in and complete the required admission process.

Surgical Assessment

Your surgical and anaesthesia teams review your condition and the planned procedure.

Preparation

You are prepared for the operating room according to the hospital's protocol.

Transfer to Operating Room

You are taken to the operating area when the team is ready.

04

During the Procedure

The exact surgical technique depends on the injury, the structures involved and the surgeon's assessment.

1. Accessing the Injury

The surgeon carefully accesses the injured area to identify the damaged tendon and surrounding structures.

2. Identifying the Tendon Ends

The damaged tendon ends are located and prepared for repair.

3. Tendon Repair

The tendon ends are carefully brought together using a surgical repair technique selected by the surgeon.

4. Wound Closure

Once the repair and other required work are completed, the surgical wound is closed and dressed.

05

Possible Complications

Every operation has potential risks. Your surgeon should explain the risks that are particularly relevant to your injury and procedure.

Infection

Infection can occur after surgery and may require medical treatment.

Stiffness

Finger and wrist movement can become limited, particularly during the healing period.

Adhesions

Scar tissue can sometimes affect the smooth movement of a repaired tendon.

Re-injury

A repaired tendon can be vulnerable while it heals, making protection and rehabilitation particularly important.

Important:

Seek medical advice promptly if your healthcare team has told you to watch for signs such as increasing pain, swelling, redness, discharge, fever or sudden loss of movement.

06

Dressing & Wound Care

Your hand may initially be protected with a dressing or splint. Follow the instructions given by your surgical and rehabilitation team.

1

Keep the dressing protected

Follow your team's instructions about keeping the dressing clean and dry.

2

Protect the repaired tendon

Avoid movements or activities that your rehabilitation team has restricted.

3

Attend dressing appointments

Attend scheduled appointments so the wound and healing process can be checked.

07

Recovery & Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation is a major part of recovery after flexor tendon repair. Exercises and progression should be guided by your hand therapist or surgical team.

Protection

The repaired tendon needs protection while healing. A splint or controlled movement programme may be prescribed.

Hand Therapy

A hand therapist may guide specific exercises designed to support safe movement and reduce stiffness.

Gradual Progress

Activities are usually increased gradually according to healing and professional advice.

Patience

Tendon healing takes time. Recovery of movement and strength can continue over an extended period.

08

Discharge

Depending on the procedure and your condition, you may be able to leave the hospital on the same day or after a period of observation.

Pain Management

Your healthcare team explains how to manage discomfort and which medicines to take if prescribed.

Wound Instructions

You receive instructions about your dressing, wound and follow-up appointments.

Activity Restrictions

You are told which movements and activities should be avoided during recovery.

09

Follow-up

Follow-up appointments allow the healthcare team to monitor healing and adjust your rehabilitation plan.

Wound Review

The surgical wound and surrounding tissues can be checked for healing.

Movement Assessment

Your team may assess finger, hand and wrist movement as recovery progresses.

Therapy Progress

Rehabilitation exercises can be adjusted according to your progress.

Return to Activities

Your healthcare team can advise when it is appropriate to gradually return to different activities.

10

Life After Surgery

Recovery does not end when the wound has healed. Continuing rehabilitation and gradually returning to normal activities are important parts of the overall journey.

Can I use my hand normally immediately?

Usually, the repaired tendon needs protection and controlled rehabilitation before normal activities are resumed.

When can I return to work?

This depends on your occupation, the injury, healing and your healthcare team's advice.

Why is rehabilitation important?

Rehabilitation helps guide safe movement while the tendon heals and can help address stiffness and movement limitations.

What if I suddenly lose movement?

Contact your surgical or healthcare team promptly, particularly if there is a sudden change after previously having movement.

Your Patient Journey Continues

Every patient's recovery is different. Your surgeon, therapist and healthcare team are the best people to guide decisions about your individual treatment, rehabilitation and return to activities.

Use this journey as an educational guide to help you understand the general stages of care and prepare questions for your healthcare team.
